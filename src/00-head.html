Robustness — Monte-Carlo resampling & walk-forward

Monte-Carlo: the real trade R-multiples are resampled with replacement into many alternate histories. The median path (blue) and the 5–95% band show how much of the result is edge vs luck of ordering.
Walk-forward: in-sample fit (grey) vs out-of-sample performance (amber) — built from the actual trades split at 70%. If amber holds up, the edge survives unseen data.

Overfitting audit — PBO via CSCV & Deflated Sharpe

A real stop × target grid is swept through the same backtest engine, then combinatorially-symmetric cross-validation checks whether the in-sample “best” config stays good out-of-sample. PBO = probability the tuned result is overfit; the Deflated Sharpe discounts the best Sharpe for how many configs were tried and for fat tails. Honest — if too few configs trade, it says so instead of guessing.

Seasonality — time-of-day & session edge finder

Splits the backtest’s real trades by the session and UTC hour they were entered, showing win-rate (with a 95% Wilson confidence band) and average R per bucket. It tells you when this strategy actually makes money — or honestly says the sample is too thin to know.

Intelligence Lab

Four advanced engines, built transparently. Everything is deterministic and inspectable — no hidden model. Where a technique normally needs deep learning, I use a principled statistical stand-in and say so, so you always know what's really running.

1 · MCTS Alpha Generator — searches rule-space to discover strategies

A real Monte Carlo Tree Search (UCB1 selection → expansion → simulation → backprop) explores combinations of trend filters, triggers, exits and stops, scoring each by your backtest engine on the loaded data. It surfaces the best "alpha" it finds — which you can save as a strategy.

2 · Generative Market Simulator

A synthetic-market agent generates plausible alternate histories (volatility clustering, fat tails, regime shifts) and stress-tests your strategy across all of them. Statistical stand-in for a deep generative model.

3 · MCMC Adaptive Position Sizing

A Metropolis–Hastings sampler estimates the posterior of your strategy's true win-rate from its trades, then sizes positions with Bayesian fractional-Kelly — conservative when the sample is small, bolder as evidence grows.

4 · MC Dropout — uncertainty on the current read

Borrowing the Monte-Carlo-Dropout idea: the confluence is re-computed hundreds of times, each pass randomly dropping ~30% of signals. If the bias barely moves, the read is robust; if it swings wildly, it's fragile. The spread is a real confidence band on right-now.

5 · Regime Classifier

Transparent state detection from ADX, volatility rank, and trend structure. Tells you which playbook fits right now — trend-following vs mean-reversion — so you stop using the wrong tool.

6 · Learned Model Forecast

Runs your uploaded deep-learning model (Settings → Model manager) on the current features, entirely in your browser. Its output joins the confluence as one more transparent, weighted voice.

7 · Ensemble Meta-Model — everything, weighted and explained

Combines the confluence, the regime bias, your learned model, and the MC-Dropout certainty into one meta-decision — then scales its conviction by how certain the signals actually are. Fully glass-box: every component shows its score and weight.

8 · Correlation & Lead-Lag Matrix — who moves first

Pairwise return correlation across your watchlist, plus a ±8-bar cross-correlation scan showing which market tends to move first. Use it to avoid stacking correlated bets and to watch the leader for early warning.

9 · Meta-Labeling Gate — which of your signals deserve size

A secondary logistic model (López de Prado’s meta-labeling idea) learns from the first 70% of your backtest’s trades which entry conditions actually won, then is judged only on the last 30%. If the gate can’t improve out-of-sample average R, it says so — honestly.

10 · Regime Ribbon (service)

The background service’s GMM regime history as a colour ribbon — red high-vol / risk-off, green low-vol / risk-on. Honest offline state when the service isn’t running.

11 · Feature Store (service)

Live snapshot of the features the service is persisting (SQLite) — what the ML lane actually sees.

③ External data — news · calendar · order-flow BRING YOUR OWN

Live news, economic calendar, order-flow and Level-2 aren't bundled — no fake data. Paste any endpoint you have access to and the terminal fetches it through the proxy (to avoid CORS). Free / effective sources to wire in:

  • News: CryptoPanic (free tier, crypto), NewsData.io, GNews, Marketaux, Finnhub /news
  • Economic calendar: Trading Economics (free tier), FinancialModelingPrep /economic_calendar, Nasdaq Data Link
  • Crypto order-flow / OI / funding (free, effective): Binance Futures fapi/v1/openInterest & fundingRate (no key), Bybit & OKX public APIs, Coinalyze (free tier); order-book depth via Binance /depth
  • Level 2 / true footprint (stocks): needs a paid tick/L2 feed — Polygon, Databento, or dxFeed. No reliable free real-time L2 exists.
The proxy forwards these GET requests as-is (/fetch?url=…) for personal use. This passthrough can reach any URL — only wire endpoints you trust, and don't paste a key you don't want on your own machine.
